Suspension or Debarment of Bidders, Offerors or Contractors (1.4.7 NMAC)
Governs how New Mexico suspends or debars contractors from state contracts. Debarment runs 3 months to 3 years from receipt of the final order. Purpose is protection, not punishment. Took effect August 30, 2013.

Honest blanks, not invented data
Two documents genuinely carry no effective date and no statutory citation: the SPD Code of Ethics and the Small Numbers Rule. Those cells are blank on purpose — no date was manufactured to make the grid look finished. In government records, a made-up date is worse than a blank one.

Signal 3
Same label, different meaning
Most rules sit under NMAC Title 1, Chapter 4 "State Procurement." But doc 3 is Title 1, Chapter 4 "Expenditure of Public Funds" under Public Finance, and doc 5 is Chapter 5 entirely. The extraction kept them straight instead of collapsing every "Chapter 4" into one bucket.

Signal 4

Effective dates run from April 1998 (design-build) to January 2025 (IT consolidation FAQ). Several rules the state relies on today are more than 25 years old. That age spread only becomes visible once the dates are pulled into one column.

Signal 5 — Strongest

The Health Dept's urban/rural table (16) tags Bernalillo Metropolitan and Catron Rural. That predicts why the identical economic template shows Bernalillo moving 0.04% on a $7.04B base while Catron swings 34.7% on ~$21M. The Small Numbers Rule (15) is the statistical explanation. A health data standard explains an economic outlier — across two agencies, three documents.
